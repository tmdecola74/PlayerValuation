PITCHER WORKLOAD COMPONENT EXPERIMENT

Decision: retain v1.43. Neither new arm passes the specified adoption guards. All new predictions remain research-only; no production forecasts, skill estimates, playing time, parks or international settings were replaced.

What was built
Expected IP = appearance probability x conditional role mixture x (expected starts x IP/start + expected relief appearances x IP/relief appearance).
Appearance uses known MLB appearances and certified nonappearance labels. Role is a mixture of starter, swing and reliever probabilities conditional on appearing. Conditional games and start fraction estimate expected starts and relief outings. Rate models learn innings per start from pure-start seasons and innings per relief appearance from zero-start seasons. Mixed-season innings splits are not observed and are not invented.

Availability is represented by nonappearance probability and reduced conditional usage. The descriptive usage proxy divides predicted games by an earlier role-specific 90th-percentile games reference. It is not a direct estimate of injury probability, healthy-season capacity, days available or a confirmed roster assignment. The experiment lacks a complete dated historical injury/roster panel. Older injuries can affect historical usage, but their causes cannot be separated from opportunity in annual totals.

Two architecture-matched arms
STRUCTURED_MLB uses prior MLB workloads, prior role, outing length, missingness and history.
STRUCTURED_MLB_MINOR adds prior minor and combined annual workloads, peaks and upper-level exposure fractions. These are physical innings, not translated talent statistics. Games counted in MLB and MiLB are disjoint. No Steamer features, target outcomes, park adjustments or international translation factors enter the model.

The initial ingestion audit stopped before fitting because collapsed organization labels repeat at DSL/CPX/R levels. Those levels were excluded before scoring, with every excluded source row preserved. Eligible AAA/AA/A+/A/A- keys are unique. Observed combined workloads are therefore lower bounds, and absence of a minor row is not a certified zero career workload. Source-availability and missing-record features remain explicit. The initial incomplete ingestion folder is not a model result; this completed release is the definitive experiment output.

Validation design
Fixed penalty 10, no hyperparameter search. Each historical origin fits scalers and models only to earlier target seasons. 2023 uses 2021–2022 training targets, 2024 uses 2021–2023, 2025 uses 2021–2024. The 2020 shortened target season was excluded from fitting by specification before scoring. Baseline history retains its original 2020 exposure; 2020 history has a source-season indicator. MiLB 2020 is unavailable, not a zero workload. Three supported scored years are available, so this is a small development backtest rather than independent confirmation. All years through 2025 were previously reviewed.

Scoring uses the same MLB-history baseline players and eligible actual labels per metric for all arms. Unknown actuals remain excluded. Certified nonappearance supplies G/OUTS only; absent GS is not fabricated. Thus starts have fewer eligible rows than innings. Minor-only players are listed as coverage exceptions without manufactured MLB opportunity.

The original workload guards retain at least three folds, >=30 players per main fold, >=1% mean innings MSE improvement, last-two-year <=2% degradation, and supported low-history <=2% degradation. This experiment additionally requires prior-role/freshness subgroup protection and G/GS safeguards. No thresholds were weakened. Equal-year MSE ratios are averaged; they are not pooled errors or percentage changes in RMSE.

Results
MLB-only components: mean innings MSE 1.42% worse than v1.43.
MLB plus minor workload: mean innings MSE 0.46% better than v1.43, below the required 1% improvement. Prior-year starter MSE is 5.32% worse and the starts guard fails. Low-history MSE is 1.22% worse, within its 2% guard but not evidence of improvement for newer players.
Adding minor workload to the component architecture improves innings MSE by 1.68% on average relative to that same architecture without minor inputs. This incremental signal does not make the overall method adoptable. Minor contribution is positive in 2023/2024 and slightly negative in 2025. The full model is worse than the baseline in 2023 and better in 2024/2025; one good late fold does not erase the guards.

Individual mechanics
The six supplied pitchers were not used to set weights, bounds or model selection. Their 2026 research predictions are shown without 2026 actuals or Steamer scores. The minor-input version raises Skenes from 164.2 baseline IP to 171.2. It lowers Gausman to 124.8, Webb to 137.0, Gilbert to 107.7, Kirby to 96.7 and Ragans to 53.8. This is not a correction to publish: the model can be too conservative for established starters. Its predicted starts/conditional usage are materially lower, so matching one young pitcher better is not sufficient. See six_example_workload_trace.csv and six_example_prior_workload_inputs.csv for exact decomposition. This report makes no claim about these predictions' 2026 accuracy.

Final freezes and future files
After historical gates, the final component coefficients were fit to 2021–2025 target seasons and frozen. research_2026_workload_unscored.csv uses 2022–2025 player history with those coefficients; it is a retrospective research forecast, not a new untouched 2026 test. Only after the decision and coefficient freeze were the registered 2026 labels opened, solely as 2027 player history. research_2027_workload.csv uses 2023–2026 history without refitting. Every original 2026 baseline IP was replayed exactly. Original v1.43 and minor-history research files remain hash-identical.

Neither future file is a complete coherent replacement stat line. Changing IP, G or GS would require a separately reviewed integration with skill rates and opportunity-dependent counts. Saves, holds, wins, quality starts and international exceptions are not silently recomputed. Ranked 2027 exceptions retain explicit unknown injury/roster status and post-freeze inherited context; those metadata were not predictors.

Recommended next research
Keep the present method frozen as a failed research candidate. A separately specified follow-up should address starter opportunity/availability calibration using dated preseason rotation and injury information and game-level start/relief innings splits. If such inputs cannot be acquired historically, the honest alternative is an explicitly assumption-based workload scenario alongside the unchanged statistical forecast. Do not hand-raise the six examples, copy Steamer innings, or tune on 2026 errors. Any revised arm needs the same historical guards and a separate specification.
