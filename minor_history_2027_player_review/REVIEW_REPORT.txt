2027 MINOR-HISTORY CHALLENGER: PLAYER REVIEW
Decision: retain v1.43 as the default. Keep the tested minor-history challenger as a separate research release. Specify and run the robustness experiment before reconsidering adoption; no production replacement is made.

Why: historical gates passed (objective MSE ratios minor/MLB-only 0.935947 hitters and 0.982016 pitchers), and the component arithmetic and source attribution reconcile. This review nevertheless identifies a source-year normalization mechanism that changes the effective population-prior weight when minor seasons are added. Its effect can be substantial for particular primitive components even with small minor samples. The prior historical test evaluates the whole method and does not isolate this mechanism. This is not proof of worse predictions, an arithmetic defect, or grounds for selective player overrides.

Coverage and ranking
All 2,511 player-role rows reviewed computationally; 10,304 primitive component rates replayed against the frozen release. There are 233 players exceeding at least one existing review threshold (HR 8, AVG .025, OPS .080, SO 35, ERA .75, WHIP .15). Only three also meet this review's substantial exposure screen (200 PA or 100 IP). These are triage thresholds, not new adoption gates. Severity ranks prioritize retirement, nonprimary roles, sparse/injury/short-stint changes, then material changes and contextual flags. A flag is not a proven error. Counts overlap. Official metadata was checked for 66 distinct player IDs, not the full universe; full case evidence is in reviewed_cases.csv.

Normalization diagnostic
The baseline uses the mean recency weight across observed MLB seasons; the challenger uses the union of MLB and minor seasons. Adding a new minor year can therefore change the effective prior strength applied to the old MLB history, even as that new year's sample approaches zero. The diagnostic holds the old mean fixed to separate direct minor evidence from the prior-normalization effect. It leaves the release untouched. There are 328 rows with a material normalization flag in at least one primitive component; that count does not establish 328 bad projections, and maximum primitive shares do not describe aggregate OPS/ERA changes. Source-specific normalization is a proposed experiment, not an adopted correction.

International and roster exceptions
The existing 118 player-role international-gap flags remain intact: 39 hitters and 79 pitchers. This review does not create international estimates. The four P1 verified identities, posting/contract evidence and ten-returner status ledger remain in the original research release, preserved verbatim. Verified identity or reported agreement does not validate a translation or establish workload. NPB/KBO coverage stays explicitly unvalidated; no minor coefficients, weaker gates, or roster assumptions fill the gap. The four returner flags present among released player-role rows remain visible. Winter assignments, active API profiles and unconfirmed agreements are not guaranteed MLB opportunities.

Limits and decision discipline
This review diagnoses individual changes; it does not score 2026 MLB outcomes, refit factors, revise priors, adjust PA/IP, enable park context, translate international performance or hand-correct players. The historical evidence still supports the tested method at the population level. Retaining v1.43 pending a separately specified robustness check is a release recommendation based on unresolved individual-level sensitivity, not a claim that the historical gates failed. A future arm must satisfy the original guards without retuning on 2026 errors.
